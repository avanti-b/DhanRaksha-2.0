What this system does

DhanRaksha scores card transactions for fraud risk. A transaction is preprocessed with the exact pipeline fitted during training, scored by a Random Forest classifier, then passed to a deterministic Risk Engine that combines the model probability with transaction-level rules derived from the training data. The result is persisted, and high-risk results open a fraud case for review.

1

Ingestion

Transaction arrives at POST /api/v1/predict and is validated before anything else runs.

2

Feature processing

The saved FeaturePipeline scales Time and Amount using statistics fitted on the training split only. PCA components pass through untouched.

3

Model

Random Forest returns a calibrated-by-vote fraud probability. No probability is ever fabricated by the application.

4

Risk engine

Deterministic rules add explainable risk on top of the model score and classify LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH.

5

Persistence

The scored transaction is written to the local database. Analytics and history read only from those records.

6

Case management

HIGH-risk transactions open a fraud case that an analyst can review, assign and resolve.

Scope and limitations
This is a student research project, not a production banking fraud system. It is trained on a single public, anonymised dataset of European card transactions from 2013 whose features are PCA components, so nothing here generalises to live payment traffic. There is no customer identity, device, geography or transaction history available, so no behavioural signals are computed — the Risk Engine exposes a seam for them rather than inventing them. Metrics are measured on one held-out split with a fixed random seed and are not cross-validated.
